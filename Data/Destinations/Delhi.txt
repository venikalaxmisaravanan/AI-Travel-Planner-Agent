Short Introduction

Delhi is India's capital and one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities, blending Mughal-era monuments, British colonial architecture, and modern urban infrastructure. It is famous for landmarks like the Red Fort, Qutub Minar, Humayun's Tomb, and India Gate, several of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. As a political, cultural, and culinary hub, Delhi offers a mix of history, government institutions, markets, and diverse regional cuisines from across India. It suits history enthusiasts, food lovers, shoppers, and first-time visitors to India, since it often serves as an international gateway city. Its scale and traffic can be overwhelming, so it is best suited to travelers comfortable with dense urban environments.

---

Best Time to Visit

- Best months: October to March
- Weather: Winters (Nov-Feb) are cool and pleasant (5-20°C); summers (Apr-Jun) are extremely hot (35-45°C) and dry; monsoon (Jul-Sep) brings humidity and moderate rain
- Festivals: Republic Day (Jan 26), Holi (Mar), Diwali (Oct/Nov), Independence Day (Aug 15)

---

Recommended Trip Duration

- 3-4 Days

This allows time to cover Old Delhi, New Delhi's monuments, museums, and at least one day for markets and day-trip flexibility (e.g., Agra).

Top Tourist Attractions

Red Fort
Description: Mughal-era fortified palace, UNESCO World Heritage Site
Time required: 2-3 hours
Entry fee: Rs 35 (Indians), Rs 550 (foreigners), approximate
Best visiting time: Morning

Qutub Minar
Description: 73-meter tall minaret built in 1193, UNESCO site
Time required: 1-2 hours
Entry fee: Rs 35 (Indians), Rs 550 (foreigners), approximate
Best visiting time: Morning or late afternoon

Humayun's Tomb
Description: 16th-century Mughal tomb, precursor to Taj Mahal design
Time required: 1.5-2 hours
Entry fee: Rs 35 (Indians), Rs 550 (foreigners), approximate
Best visiting time: Morning

India Gate
Description: War memorial arch, popular evening gathering spot
Time required: 30-60 minutes
Entry fee: Free
Best visiting time: Evening

Jama Masjid
Description: One of India's largest mosques, built by Shah Jahan
Time required: 1 hour
Entry fee: Free (camera fee may apply)
Best visiting time: Morning, avoid prayer times

Akshardham Temple
Description: Large modern Hindu temple complex with exhibitions
Time required: 3-4 hours
Entry fee: Free (exhibition tickets Rs 170-250 approximate)
Best visiting time: Afternoon/evening (light show at night)

Lotus Temple
Description: Bahai House of Worship shaped like a lotus flower
Time required: 45-60 minutes
Entry fee: Free
Best visiting time: Morning (fewer crowds)

Hidden Gems

- Hauz Khas Village: Combines a medieval reservoir and madrasa ruins with cafes and boutique shops - a blend of history and nightlife
- Mehrauli Archaeological Park: Lesser-visited park with scattered ruins spanning multiple dynasties, quieter than Qutub Minar
- Nizamuddin Dargah: Sufi shrine with Qawwali singing on Thursday evenings, offering cultural immersion
- Agrasen ki Baoli: A striking stepwell in the middle of the city, often overlooked by tourists
- Sunder Nursery: A restored heritage park near Humayun's Tomb, good for a peaceful walk

Chaat (Golgappa/Aloo Tikki)

Parathas
Description: Stuffed flatbreads served with pickle and curd
Recommended Area: Paranthe Wali Gali, Chandni Chowk

Kebabs
Description: Grilled meat skewers, Mughlai-style
Recommended Area: Jama Masjid area

Chole Bhature
Description: Spiced chickpeas with fried bread

Shopping

Popular markets: Chandni Chowk (spices, textiles, jewelry), Sarojini Nagar (clothing, budget fashion), Dilli Haat (handicrafts from across India), Khan Market (upscale boutiques)
What to buy: Textiles, spices, jewelry, leather goods, handicrafts
Approximate price range: Rs 100-2,000+ depending on item
Bargaining tips: Bargaining is common and expected in markets like Chandni Chowk and Sarojini Nagar; fixed-price shops (Dilli Haat stalls, malls) generally do not allow bargaining

Transportation

How to reach:
Nearest airport: Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL)
Railway station: New Delhi Railway Station, Old Delhi Railway Station, Nizamuddin Railway Station
Bus connectivity: Well connected via interstate bus terminals (ISBT) to major North Indian cities

Local transport:
Metro availability: Yes, extensive network covering most tourist areas
Taxi: Widely available, including app-based (Ola, Uber)
Auto: Common; metered or negotiated
Bike rentals: Available in some areas, less common than other cities
Public buses: DTC buses cover the city extensively

Safety Tips

- Areas to avoid: Isolated areas late at night, particularly in outer Delhi
- Night travel advice: Prefer app-based cabs over unmarked taxis after dark; avoid traveling alone in secluded areas at night
- Emergency numbers: Police: 100, Ambulance: 102, Tourist Helpline: 1363
- General safety recommendations: Keep valuables secure in crowded markets; use registered transport; women travellers should take extra precautions at night

---

Common Tourist Scams

- Inflated auto/taxi fares: Insist on using the meter or app-based booking
- Fake tour agents near tourist sites: Book tours only through verified/government-approved operators
- Gem and "government emporium" scams: Be wary of touts directing tourists to specific shops claiming special deals
- Overcharging at markets: Bargain and compare prices before purchasing

---

Travel Tips

- Best season: October to March
- What to pack: Light cottons for summer, warm layers for winter (December-January can get cold, 5-8°C)
- Local etiquette: Dress modestly at religious sites; remove shoes before entering temples/mosques
- Language: Hindi and English widely spoken
- Cash vs UPI: UPI (digital payments) widely accepted in shops and with vendors; cash still useful for small street vendors
- Network availability: Good 4G/5G coverage across the city
- Medical facilities: Well-developed; multiple multi-specialty hospitals (AIIMS, Apollo, Max, Fortis)

Interesting Facts

1. Delhi has been the capital of several empires over more than 1,000 years, including the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal Empire.
2. The city contains three UNESCO World Heritage Sites: Red Fort, Qutub Minar, and Humayun's Tomb.
3. Delhi's Metro is one of the largest metro networks in the world.
4. The Qutub Minar is the tallest brick minaret in the world.
5. Delhi is often called a city of "Seven Cities" due to historical layers of settlements built by different rulers.
6. India Gate was built as a memorial to soldiers who died in World War I.
7. Chandni Chowk was designed in the 17th century by Jahanara, Shah Jahan's daughter.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is 3 days enough for Delhi? Yes, 3-4 days is generally sufficient to cover major sites.
2. Is Delhi safe for solo travellers? Yes, with standard urban precautions, especially at night.
3. Which month is best to visit Delhi? October to March, due to cooler weather.
4. What food should I try in Delhi? Butter chicken, chaat, parathas, and kebabs.
5. What should I avoid in Delhi? Unmetered taxis, unofficial tour guides, and isolated areas at night.
6. Is Delhi Metro tourist-friendly? Yes, it is extensive, affordable, and connects most major attractions.
7. Do I need a guide for Red Fort or Qutub Minar? Not mandatory; audio guides are available, though hiring a guide can add historical context.
8. Is Delhi good for shopping? Yes, with markets ranging from budget (Sarojini Nagar) to upscale (Khan Market).
9. Can I visit Agra as a day trip from Delhi? Yes, via train or expressway, roughly 3-4 hours each way.
10. Is tap water safe to drink in Delhi? No, bottled or filtered water is recommended.
11. What is the dress code for religious sites? Modest clothing; head covering may be required in some mosques/gurudwaras.
12. Is Delhi expensive? It can suit a range of budgets, from budget hostels to luxury 5-star hotels.
13. How polluted is Delhi? Air quality often worsens in winter (Nov-Jan); sensitive travellers should check AQI levels.
14. Is haggling expected at markets? Yes, particularly at Chandni Chowk and Sarojini Nagar.
15. What is the best way to reach Delhi from other Indian cities? Air, rail, and road connections are all extensive and well-developed.
