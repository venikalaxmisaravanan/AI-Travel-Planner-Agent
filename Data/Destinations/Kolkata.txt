City Name: Kolkata

State / Union Territory: West Bengal

Region of India: East India

---

Short Introduction

Kolkata, formerly known as Calcutta, was the capital of British India until 1911 and remains a major cultural and intellectual center of East India. The city is known for its colonial-era architecture, including the Victoria Memorial and Howrah Bridge, its literary and artistic heritage, and strong traditions in classical music, theatre, and cinema. Kolkata is unique for its blend of British colonial history with deep-rooted Bengali cultural identity, including its renowned sweets and Durga Puja celebrations. It suits history and literature enthusiasts, food lovers, and travelers interested in India's colonial past and Bengali cultural traditions.

Best Time to Visit

- Best months: October to February
- Weather: Winters (Nov-Feb) are mild and pleasant (12-27°C); summers (Mar-Jun) are hot and humid (28-40°C); monsoon (Jun-Sep) brings heavy rainfall
- Festivals: Durga Puja (Sep/Oct, the city's signature festival), Kali Puja/Diwali

Top Tourist Attractions

Victoria Memorial
Description: Grand white marble monument built in memory of Queen Victoria, now housing a museum and gardens
Time required: 2 hours
Entry fee: Rs 30 (Indians), Rs 500 (foreigners), approximate
Best visiting time: Morning to afternoon

Howrah Bridge
Description: Iconic cantilever bridge over the Hooghly River, one of the busiest bridges in the world
Time required: 30-45 minutes (viewing)
Entry fee: Free
Best visiting time: Sunset or early morning

Dakshineswar Kali Temple
Description: 19th-century temple dedicated to Goddess Kali, associated with saint Ramakrishna Paramahamsa
Time required: 1-1.5 hours
Entry fee: Free
Best visiting time: Morning

Indian Museum
Description: One of the oldest and largest museums in India, housing art, archaeology, and natural history collections
Time required: 2 hours
Entry fee: Rs 50 (Indians), Rs 500 (foreigners), approximate
Best visiting time: Morning to afternoon

St. Paul's Cathedral
Description: 19th-century Anglican cathedral with Gothic Revival architecture
Time required: 45-60 minutes
Entry fee: Free
Best visiting time: Morning

---

Hidden Gems

- College Street: A historic book market street, home to the University of Calcutta and countless secondhand bookstores
- Kumartuli: A traditional potters' quarter where artisans create clay idols, especially active before Durga Puja
- Marble Palace: A 19th-century mansion housing art and antiques, requiring advance permission to visit
- Princep Ghat: A riverside colonial monument offering a quieter spot along the Hooghly River

---

Local Food to Try

Kolkata Biryani
Description: Fragrant biryani distinct for its use of potato alongside meat, a Kolkata specialty
Recommended Area: Arsalan, Aminia

Rosogolla and Sandesh
Description: Classic Bengali sweets made from chhena (a type of cottage cheese)
Recommended Area: K.C. Das, local sweet shops citywide

Kathi Rolls
Description: Flatbread rolls filled with grilled meat or paneer and vegetables, originating in Kolkata
Recommended Area: Nizam's, local street stalls

Machher Jhol (Fish Curry)
Description: Traditional Bengali fish curry, often made with mustard-based gravy
Recommended Area: Local Bengali restaurants citywide

Shopping

Popular markets: New Market, Gariahat Market, College Street
What to buy: Bengali handloom sarees (Tant, Jamdani), books, terracotta items, leather goods
Approximate price range: Rs 150-4,000+ depending on item
Bargaining tips: Bargaining is common at New Market and Gariahat Market, particularly for textiles and general goods

---

Transportation

How to reach:
Nearest airport: Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport (CCU)
Railway station: Howrah Junction and Sealdah, major railway hubs connecting to all parts of India
Bus connectivity: Extensive state and private bus network connecting to other East Indian cities

Local transport:
Metro availability: Yes, Kolkata Metro is India's oldest metro system, covering key parts of the city
Taxi: Widely available, including app-based options and iconic yellow taxis
Auto: Common in suburban areas
Bike rentals: Limited availability
Public buses: Extensive local bus network; trams also operate in parts of the city, among the oldest in Asia

Nearby Places Worth Visiting

- Digha (approx. 185 km): Popular beach town on the Bay of Bengal
- Shantiniketan (approx. 160 km): Cultural town founded by Rabindranath Tagore, home to Visva-Bharati University

---

Festivals and Cultural Experiences

- Durga Puja (Sep/Oct): Kolkata's largest and most significant festival, with elaborately decorated pandals across the city
- Kali Puja (Oct/Nov): Celebrated around the same period as Diwali, with a focus on Goddess Kali
- Poila Boishakh (Bengali New Year, Apr): Celebrated with cultural programs and traditional food

---

Safety Tips

- Areas to avoid: Overcrowded areas during Durga Puja processions late at night
- Night travel advice: Use registered taxis or app-based cabs after dark
- Emergency numbers: Police: 100, Ambulance: 108, Tourist Helpline: 1363
- General safety recommendations: Keep valuables secure in crowded markets and during festival crowds

---

Common Tourist Scams

- Overpriced "handloom" saree claims: Purchase from established, reputed textile shops
- Inflated taxi fares for airport transfers: Use metered taxis or app-based booking
- Unofficial guides at Victoria Memorial charging high fees: Audio guides are available through official counters
- Overcharging at street food stalls for tourists: Confirm prices before ordering, especially in high-footfall tourist areas

---

Travel Tips

- Best season: October to February
- What to pack: Light cottons for most of the year, light woolens for winter mornings/evenings, rain gear during monsoon
- Local etiquette: Modest dress recommended at religious sites
- Language: Bengali, Hindi, and English widely spoken
- Cash vs UPI: UPI widely accepted in shops; cash useful for smaller vendors and street food stalls
- Network availability: Good 4G coverage across the city
- Medical facilities: Well-developed; multiple multi-specialty hospitals available

Interesting Facts

1. Kolkata served as the capital of British India until the capital was shifted to Delhi in 1911.
2. The city was renamed from Calcutta to Kolkata in 2001, reflecting the Bengali pronunciation.
3. Kolkata's tram network is one of the oldest in Asia, having operated since the 19th century.
4. The Victoria Memorial took nearly 15 years to complete, finished in 1921.
5. Kolkata is often referred to as the "cultural capital of India" due to its rich literary, artistic, and cinematic heritage.
6. Rosogolla, one of Bengal's most famous sweets, has been granted a Geographical Indication (GI) tag associated with West Bengal.
7. Kolkata's Durga Puja celebrations were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2021.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is 2 days enough for Kolkata? Yes, though 3 days allows more time for markets and cultural sites like College Street.
2. Is Kolkata safe for solo travellers? Yes, it is generally considered safe with standard urban precautions.
3. Which month is best to visit Kolkata? October to February, for pleasant weather; September/October for Durga Puja.
4. What food should I try in Kolkata? Kolkata biryani, rosogolla, and kathi rolls.
5. What should I avoid in Kolkata? Unverified "handloom" saree claims and unofficial museum guides.
6. Is visiting during Durga Puja worth it? Yes, it offers an unmatched cultural experience, though the city is significantly more crowded during this time.
7. Is Kolkata good for a family trip? Yes, museums and heritage sites are suitable for visitors of all ages.
8. Is tap water safe to drink in Kolkata? No, bottled or filtered water is recommended.
9. What is the best way to reach Kolkata? Well connected by air, rail, and road from all major Indian cities.
10. Is the Kolkata Metro tourist-friendly? Yes, it is India's oldest metro system and connects several key areas of the city.
11. Is Kolkata good for shopping? Yes, particularly for handloom sarees at Gariahat Market and books at College Street.
12. Is haggling expected at markets? Yes, particularly at New Market and Gariahat Market.
13. What is the dress code for religious sites in Kolkata? Modest clothing is recommended.
14. Is Kolkata a good base for exploring nearby West Bengal? Yes, it serves as a gateway to the Sundarbans, Digha, and Shantiniketan.
15. What is the best time to visit Howrah Bridge? Sunset, for scenic views and a lively riverside atmosphere.
